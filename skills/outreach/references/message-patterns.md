# Message patterns

Load when drafting a message. Every person, company and detail below is invented. Use the shape, rewrite every word from the real evidence and the real recipient. The candidate in the examples is "Jordan Example"; the companies are "Northstar" and "Fieldwork".

## Before drafting

Write down three things. If any is missing, find it or ask.

1. The specific reason this person is the right recipient (verified).
2. The one fact about the candidate that matters to them (from an evidence file).
3. The ask, in one sentence, answerable in two minutes.

## Hiring manager or team lead

Reason: their team's work. Ask: a pointed question, or a short conversation.

> Hi Riley, I read your team's post on moving reconciliation from nightly batches to continuous matching. I just applied for the Backend Engineer role on the ledger team.
>
> Last year I traced a weekly duplicate-invoice problem to a retry path and built the comparison job that caught it, so the "is this mismatch real" question in your post is one I have lived with.
>
> Is the role mostly on the matching rules or on the pipeline around them? Happy to share more if useful, and no worries if this is not the right moment.
>
> Jordan Example

Why it works: a reason only this recipient could receive, one proof line tied to their problem, one question they can answer in a sentence, an easy out. About 95 words.

## Recruiter on the requisition

Recruiters sort. Give them what they need to sort the candidate in: role, the two strongest matching facts, logistics that matter.

> Hi Avery, I applied this week for Backend Engineer, Ledger (req 4821) at Northstar.
>
> Two things that map to the posting: I own a payment reconciliation path in production, including on-call, and I shipped billing features inside a forty-engineer codebase before that. I am based in the same time zone as the team and can start in June.
>
> If you are the right person for this role, I would be glad to send anything that helps. If not, could you point me to who is?
>
> Jordan Example

Do not ask a recruiter for status in a first message. Do not paste the resume into the message.

## Peer on the team

Ask about the work, not for a favor. People answer questions about what they do.

> Hi Morgan, I saw you joined Northstar's ledger team last year after working on billing at a smaller company. I am considering the same move and applied for the Backend Engineer role there.
>
> I have been building a reconciliation job on a much smaller ledger. What surprised you most about the problem at Northstar's scale?
>
> No pressure to reply. Thanks either way.
>
> Jordan Example

If they reply and the exchange goes well, a later message can ask who is hiring for the role. Do not ask for a referral in the first message.

## Shared background, different function

The shared background is the whole reason. Say plainly that the role may sit outside their area.

> Hi Casey, I noticed we both studied at <school>. I applied for the Backend Engineer role on Northstar's ledger team. I know you work in finance recruiting, so this may sit outside your area.
>
> If you know who is handling that search, I would appreciate a pointer.
>
> Jordan Example

No school slogans, no nostalgia, no claim that they can route the application.

## Connection-request note

Reason plus ask. Nothing else fits.

> Hi Riley, your post on continuous matching is close to what I work on. I applied for Backend Engineer on your team and would value connecting. Jordan

## Warm introduction: a blurb for the introducer to forward

Write it so the introducer can paste it without editing. Third person or first person, as the introducer prefers. Three lines.

> Jordan Example is a backend engineer who built and runs the payment reconciliation path at Fieldwork. They have applied for the Backend Engineer role on Northstar's ledger team (link). Jordan would value fifteen minutes with someone on that team to understand the work.

And the note to the introducer:

> Thanks for offering to introduce me to Riley. A short blurb is below so you can forward it as is, and my resume is attached. If the timing is not right for Riley, I completely understand.

Mention the personal connection in one light sentence only if the introducer said it matters. It is context, not a qualification.

## Referral ask to someone who knows the work

> Hi Sam, Northstar has a Backend Engineer opening on the ledger team that fits what we worked on together at Fieldwork. Would you be comfortable referring me?
>
> To make it easy: the posting is here (link), and a blurb you can paste is below. If you would rather not, or the timing is bad, that is completely fine.
>
> "Jordan and I worked together on billing at Fieldwork. Jordan found and fixed the duplicate-invoice problem and built the reconciliation job we still run. I would be glad to work with them again."

Draft the blurb only from facts the referrer witnessed. They are putting their name on it.

## Follow-up to an unanswered message

Same thread. Add something new. Three sentences.

> Hi Riley, a short follow-up to my note last week. Since then I have submitted the application and published a write-up of the reconciliation job (link), in case it is useful context. Still glad to hear whether the role leans toward the matching rules or the pipeline.

Second and final follow-up, about a week later, shorter:

> Hi Riley, last note from me on this. If someone else on the team is a better person to ask, I would be grateful for the name. Thanks for your time either way.

Then stop and set the person to `paused`.

## Check-in on a quiet application

To the recruiter or coordinator, after the configured gap.

> Hi Avery, I applied for Backend Engineer, Ledger (req 4821) on 3 March and remain very interested. Since applying I have <one new fact, if any>. Is there anything further I can provide, or a timeline I should expect?

If the employer gave a date, wait until it has passed before sending this.

## Reply to an inbound recruiter

Answer their questions in their order. Offer concrete times.

> Hi Avery, thanks for reaching out. Yes, I am interested in the Backend Engineer role.
>
> To your questions: I am available to start in June, and I am authorized to work in <country> <as the profile states>. I can talk Tuesday 10:00 to 12:00 or Thursday after 14:00, Eastern time.
>
> Jordan Example

Declining:

> Hi Avery, thank you for thinking of me. This one is not the right direction for me at the moment, since I am focused on backend roles. I would be glad to hear from you if something on the ledger side opens up.

## Thank-you after an interview

Within a day. One specific moment, one thing added, no re-pitch.

> Hi Riley, thank you for the conversation today. Your point about late records looking identical to wrong ones for the first hour stayed with me. On your question about replaying history: I checked, and our replay covered 60 days, not 30 as I said. I enjoyed the discussion and would be glad to continue it.
>
> Jordan Example

For a panel, each note references a different moment. A correction of something said in the interview belongs here, stated plainly.

## Subject lines for email

- Name the role or the reason: "Backend Engineer, Ledger: a question about continuous matching".
- For a follow-up, reply in the thread and keep the subject.
- Never "Quick question", "Following up", "Opportunity", or anything that hides who is writing and why.

## Tone repairs

| Draft says | Problem | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "I would love to pick your brain" | Vague, costly ask | The one question |
| "I know you are very busy, but" | Opens with an apology | Start with the reason |
| "I am passionate about your mission" | Unverifiable praise | The specific thing they did |
| "Any advice would be greatly appreciated" | No answerable ask | "Is the role mostly X or Y?" |
| "I believe I would be a great fit" | Claim without evidence | The one proof line |
| "Please let me know if there are any openings" | Asks them to do the search | Name the role already found |
